Online wildlife-trade monitoring is a textbook “needle-in-a-haystack” problem, requiring large volumes of adverts to be covered while keeping missed detections, manual review and costs manageable. This study evaluates whether off-the-shelf decision models can competitively perform this task through policy prompt changes alone without task-specific training. Three decision models were compared with a contemporary chat LLM across three historical monitoring datasets gathered from the literature, alongside previously published LLM and supervised classifier benchmarks. Across zero-shot experimental conditions, decision models averaged 93.2% balanced accuracy compared with 90.3% for the chat model, at lower average request latency (~4.85 minutes per 1,000 adverts) and API costs (~$0.02 per 1,000 adverts). Comparisons with selected published supervised baselines also suggested strong performance, although differences in data, inputs and evaluation methods limit their interpretation. The locally hosted, 4-bit quantised `d3` model was particularly consistent, averaging ~96.0% (±0.19 pp) balanced accuracy across datasets with no training, threshold calibration, or in-context examples. These findings support off-the-shelf decision models as accurate, low-cost wildlife-advert screeners that can be adapted to new monitoring policies through pure natural language specification.
