Across three historical wildlife-advert datasets, off-the-shelf decision models delivered competitive screening performance through policy prompt changes alone, with low hosted API costs and strong inference speed. The locally deployed `d3` model was particularly consistent, matching or exceeding baselines under zero-shot conditions. All in all, these findings support decision models as a practical direction for wildlife-trade monitoring, enabling low-cost policy adaptation through natural language specification. However, prospective evaluation is needed to establish whether this translates into manageable review workloads, lower total operating costs and useful investigative leads in practice.
