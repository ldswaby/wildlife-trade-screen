### Screening performance

**At the fixed 0.5 cutoff, decision models outperformed selected published baselines through zero-shot policy changes alone.** Measured decision-model averages were 96.22% on Shark Products and 89.2% on Animal Products, compared with estimated published reference values of 94.1% for `ACTL-text` and 87.7% for `LTS-text`, respectively ([Figure 3](#summary-figure-title)). These results are conditional on [confounds](#limitations) that preclude a controlled comparison, such as restructured inputs and undisclosed metric conventions. No matching published baseline exists for the combined Live Birds policy.

**The contemporary chat-LLM baseline also generally fell below zero-shot decision-model dataset averages.** Specifically, zero-shot decision models averaged 93.19% (±5.88 pp) balanced accuracy across all tasks, compared with 90.25% (±7.32 pp) for `GPT-6 Luna Chat`, weighting datasets and decision models equally and supplying as much advert info as possible per dataset ([Figure 3](#summary-figure-title)). These averages conceal a handful of exceptions where higher TPR came with substantially higher FPR. For example, FPR was 40.2–44.2% for the `Luna` models on Animal Products ([Figure 1](#figure-1?dataset=animals&models=all&prompts=zero-shot&inputs=title)) and 15.5% for `Microsoft-Decision-1` on Live Birds ([Figure 1](#figure-1?dataset=birds&models=all&prompts=zero-shot&inputs=title_and_desc)). Decision models therefore did not consistently outperform `GPT-6 Luna Chat` on every dataset. Published `GPT-4` F1 was also numerically competitive on the Shark Products task, scoring 94.6% compared to decision models' average 92.33% (±0.76 pp) and `GPT-6 Luna Chat`'s 94.9% (±0 pp) with few-shot prompting ([Table 1](#table-1?dataset=sharks&group=input)).

**Among decision models, no model consistently led across all tasks.** However, *perhaps most striking was the consistency of the locally hosted 4-bit quantised `d3` model, which achieved 95.96% (±0.19 pp) average balanced accuracy through zero-shot policy changes alone* ([Figure 3](#summary-figure-title)). Its observed FPR @ 95% TPR ranged from 3.02% to 4.27%, giving it the lowest cross-dataset average of 3.64% (±0.5 pp), although `GPT-6 Luna Decisions` performed better on the Shark Products and Live Birds datasets (note again that this particular metric is computed retrospectively using decision thresholds identified separately on each test dataset rather than the fixed 0.5 cutoff, and therefore does not establish performance on new adverts).

Unsurprisingly, wherever the presence of separate input text fields in the data permits direct comparison (only the Shark Products dataset here), **adding advert descriptions  generally improved screening results**, reducing false positives without sacrificing recall across decision models ([Table 1](#table-1?dataset=sharks&group=prompt), [Figure 1](#figure-1?dataset=sharks&models=decision&prompts=zero-shot&inputs=title,title_and_desc)). **Few-shot examples, tested on the Shark Products and Animal Products datasets, had more mixed effects**: they substantially reduced the `Luna` models’ Animal Products FPR, sometimes at the expense of TPR ([Figure 1](#figure-1?dataset=animals&models=gpt_6_luna_chat,gpt_6_luna_decisions&prompts=zero-shot,few-shot&inputs=title)). Improvements at the default cutoff did not always translate into better high-recall trade-offs. Live Birds was evaluated only under zero-shot prompting, with no separate input comparison.


### Resource usage

**The evaluated decision and chat models had low hosted API costs.** Hosted decision screening cost approximately $0.004–$0.081 per 1,000 adverts at the configured list prices. `Microsoft-Decision-1` had the lowest hosted charges at ~$0.011 (±0.007), while, interestingly, `GPT-6 Luna Decisions` cost more on average at ~$0.034 (±0.017) than matched chat configurations, which cost on average ~$0.03 (±0.017) across zero-shot experiments ([Figure 3](#summary-figure-title)). The 4-bit quantised `d3` model used here was run locally and as such incurred no external API charge, preventing a total-affordability verdict. The evaluated Q4_K_M `d3` model ran locally on a 32 GB consumer laptop, demonstrating feasibility on accessible hardware.

**The evaluated chat configuration had substantially lower API costs than the historical GPT-4 reference.** For comparison with published baselines, our most useful reference point is [Barbosa et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211)'s estimate that at the time of writing GPT-4 would have cost over $17,000 to label 800,000 adverts (over $21.25 per 1,000 adverts), precluding its use for screening at scale. By contrast, these experiments revealed `GPT-6 Luna Chat` to be several orders of magnitude cheaper, averaging just $0.04 (±0.02) per 1,000 ads in API charges across all experiments using the same datasets ([Figure 2 - Shark Products](#figure-2?dataset=sharks&group=input,prompt), [Figure 2 - Animal Products](#figure-2?dataset=animals&group=prompt)). Note that this historical comparison does not isolate savings attributable to decision-model architecture.

**The evaluated models offered practical recorded inference times with no task-specific training.** Preparation effort and inference time capture distinct but equally important aspects of practical resource use. Preparation includes defining the monitoring policy, preparing data, configuring deployment and, for supervised classifiers, manual annotation and subsequent model training. Here, decision/chat models were applied to different policies through prompt changes without task-specific training. Although total operational savings could not be measured here, this obviation alone could entail significant workload reductions for most workflows (note, however, that decision threshold tuning would reintroduce development data and evaluation requirements). Using summed request time at the recorded settings, hosted decision models averaged 4.85 ± 1.39 minutes of summed request time per 1,000 adverts, compared with 20.79 ± 7.05 minutes for `GPT-6 Luna Chat` across the zero-shot configurations in [Figure 3](#summary-figure-title). The local `d3` model had longer recorded request times than its hosted counterparts, averaging 41.71 ± 19.74 minutes per 1,000 adverts. However, these timings reflect the local hardware and runtime used, and therefore do not entail an intrinsic speed disadvantage.

**Recorded concurrent runs screened tens of thousands of adverts in minutes.** For the 16,407-advert Live Birds dataset, simple bounded multithreading (i.e., no native batch APIs) with a concurrency of 16 allowed `GPT-6 Luna Decisions` to complete scoring in 3.6 minutes—approximately 13 seconds per 1,000 adverts—while GPT-6 Luna Chat took 20.1 minutes. These results further underscore the practical feasibility of large-scale screening with both model types.
