- **Source:** [Stringham et al. (2021), release version 2](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Text_classification_to_streamline_online_wildlife_trade_analyses/14032742/2?file=26465975).
- **Collection method:** Scraped from an anonymised Australian classifieds website between July and December 2019.
- **Labelling method:** Four authors manually assigned three separate binary exclusion labels to 16,509 samples, each corresponding to a different policy: 1. whether the ad is junk (e.g., a bird accessory such as a cage or food); 2. whether the ad presents a domestic poultry species; and 3. whether the ad requests a bird rather than lists one for sale.
- **Processing:** For this study, the three exclusion labels were collapsed into a single relevance target (`relevant` / `not relevant`): any known exclusion makes an advert negative; three explicit zeros make it positive; otherwise it remains unresolved. The resulting test dataset contains 16,407 samples with a single binary label, with 102 unresolved records dropped for these experiments. The only other changes made were column name edits and a conversion of the resulting file to UTF-8.
- **Features:** [Release version 2](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Text_classification_to_streamline_online_wildlife_trade_analyses/14032742/2?file=26465975) retains raw advert titles and descriptions (title + desc), rather than the stemmed representation used by the original classifiers.
- **Labels/policy:** The policy targets live-bird offers, excluding junk, wanted adverts and the source’s “domestic poultry” (*Anseriformes*, *Galliformes*, and domestic pigeons).
- **Caveats:** The combined relevance target is derived from the source’s three exclusion labels and has not been independently reannotated. Original annotators could use images as well as text, whereas the models evaluated here receive text only. Each advert was labelled by one annotator, so agreement between annotators was not measured.
