- **Reporting and prompting ambiguities:**  On the `Shark Products` dataset, LTS-text scored a reported F1 of **0.872 in [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S4.T2)** and **0.873 in [Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S4.T5)**, compared to an in-line cited F1 score of **0.89 ([Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S4.SS3))**, which, as far as could be ascertained, actually belongs to the multimodal model. The paper also inconsistently uses “zero-shot” and “few-shot”: [Figure 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S3.F3) includes five in-context examples complete with labels and explanations, but whether these were used for [GPT‑4’s reported shark F1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S4.T5) of **0.946** remains unclear. This ambiguity is addressed here insofar as possible by evaluating separate fixed zero-shot and few-shot conditions, but neither is a confirmed reproduction.

- **A meaningful yet limited comparison:** LTS’s trained classifiers learn from labels generated by an LLM using [near-equivalent policy prompts](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S3.F3) to those used in this study. This supports comparing direct language model screening with [their active learning pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S3.SS4). However, their exact test-file identity, prompting (e.g., in the case of their [reported ‘zero-shot’ LLM results](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S4.SS3)) and metric averaging methods remain uncertain. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S3.SS4) and [released code](https://github.com/VIDA-NYU/LTS/blob/main/main_cluster.py) also use validation performance for model selection, Thompson-sampling rewards, and [early stopping and checkpoint selection](https://github.com/VIDA-NYU/LTS/blob/main/fine_tune.py), indicating possible data leakage that can lead to inflated published scores in the absence of a distinct, untouched hold-out test set. Published LTS scores therefore provide a relevant historical reference, but the comparison cannot isolate the effect of the screening approach.

- **Dataset suitability and available evidence:** The `Leather` dataset from the LTS study was excluded because its labels could not be reliably reconciled with the stated screening policy. Positive-labelled items include apparently non-leather products, such as a python skull, while the broad animal-or-leather/skin wording in the [supplied prompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S3.F3) conflicts with the [narrower leather objective](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S4.SS1). Broader annotation scope, inconsistent labels or a dataset-version discrepancy remain possible explanations; none is established. Separately, the supplied files contain image filenames without the corresponding images, preventing multimodal reproduction. Published [text-and-image results](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21211#S4.T2) also use additional evidence and must remain distinct from text-only comparisons.

- **No matching published bird-composite baseline:** [Stringham et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0254007)’s separate exclusion classifiers were evaluated using cross-validation and task-specific eligible rows. Their published aggregates do not measure the combined relevance rule used here ([Live Birds Dataset details](#birds-dataset)), so they cannot serve as a directly comparable bird-composite baseline.

- **Published bird split-policy references:** The green × and whiskers in [Figure 3](#summary-figure-title) summarise the best published classifiers for domestic poultry, junk and wanted adverts with an equal-weight mean and ±1 population SD across those three policy values. This SD describes policy variation, not confidence intervals or cross-validation-fold variation; sample sizes are not pooled or used as weights. Their balanced accuracy is derived from rounded fold-average recall and specificity in [Stringham et al. (2021), Appendix S2](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0254007.s002). The original models used 10-fold cross-validation and different eligible samples for each policy, whereas our zero-shot models evaluate one combined relevance rule. These are contextual references, not directly matched comparisons; they cannot be pooled into a combined-policy score or used to infer our target-recall performance or resource costs.
